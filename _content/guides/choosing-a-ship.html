Quick answer

Choose a ship around an activity, the people available to crew it and the time you have to play. Compare the complete job—from boarding and loading to returning home—before comparing isolated statistics.

Start with the task you enjoy

RSI’s ship overview describes combat, transport, exploration, industrial, support, competition and multipurpose roles. A role describes an intended purpose; it does not prove that every advertised feature is implemented or that the ship suits your sessions.

Write one sentence before browsing: “I want to carry small loads with one friend,” or “I want to practise flying alone.” This gives you a reason to keep or reject a candidate without chasing a changing tier list.

Count the people who actually show up

Distinguish the number of seats from the jobs you want those people to perform. Ask who will pilot, navigate, handle cargo or operate other stations. Then consider the evening when a regular teammate is absent.

For solo play, inspect which tasks are accessible from your normal position and which require leaving it. For a group, try the boarding and handover process together. A ship that is enjoyable with a full crew may create a different workload alone.

Use a practical comparison sheet

QuestionWhat to observe during a trial
Can it do my task?Required equipment and the actual workflow, including any unavailable features.
Does the access suit me?Entry, cockpit visibility, internal movement and loading access.
Does it fit my group?Useful jobs for the people who regularly join.
Does it fit my session?Preparation, travel, task time and the return trip.
Can I sustain its use?Service needs, equipment replacement and consequences of a failed trip.

Record observations as “works for my task,” “needs another trial,” or “does not fit.” Do not turn uncertain impressions into precise scores. Two ships can make different tradeoffs without one being universally better.

Try the whole loop before deciding

If the ship is available to rent, plan a session that includes departure, a representative task and landing. Check the rental’s equipment restrictions so the comparison is fair. An available demonstration with another player can also answer access and crew questions, but agree on the plan first.

Repeat the ordinary parts. A comfortable cockpit matters, but so does whether you enjoy getting a load aboard and finishing the trip. Keep your existing ship as the reference point: what specific problem would the new one solve?

Separate the ship from game access

RSI distinguishes game packages and standalone pledges. Check the exact contents when access is the goal. For an earned-currency purchase, use the separate aUEC buying guide.

You do not need to decide on an entire fleet at once. Finish with a short list of questions a real trial can answer, rather than a shopping list based on future possibilities.
