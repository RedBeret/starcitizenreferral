Quick answer

Estimate your share of a contract reward, subtract the trip expenses you pay, and include the whole session when comparing time. This calculator uses your entries; it does not fetch live payouts, read your wallet or predict a successful mission.

The calculation, in plain language

  • Your gross share = total reward ÷ participants.
  • Your net = your gross share − your costs.
  • Your estimated hourly net = your net × 60 ÷ total minutes.

RSI’s contract guide describes sharing a mission and dividing its reward equally among the party members who accept. This tool uses that equal-split assumption. It does not model eligibility exceptions, game rounding or any additional reward components.

If you already know your personal payout, use one participant and enter that amount as the reward. Do not enter your personal share and divide it a second time.

A fictional worked example

Suppose a contract pays 30,000 aUEC in total, two people share it, you pay 3,000 aUEC in trip costs, and the full trip takes 45 minutes. Your share is 15,000 aUEC. After your costs, the net is 12,000 aUEC. At that pace the arithmetic gives 16,000 aUEC per hour.

Those numbers demonstrate the calculation. They are not a current contract offer, a normal income claim or a guarantee that another trip will produce the same result.

Compare a whole trip, not just the objective

Start your time estimate before preparation and stop after the relevant return or hand-in. If one job requires a long shopping trip while another uses equipment already on hand, that difference belongs in the comparison.

Costs should follow the comparison you want to make. Charging a full equipment purchase to the first trip gives a different picture from spreading its cost across several trips. Choose one approach, label it in your own notes and use it consistently. Do not count the same expense twice.

Understand what the estimate leaves out

The result assumes the entered reward is received. It does not price the chance of failure, a crash, lost cargo or a changed destination. RSI’s loss guidance says support does not manually restore aUEC lost through a crash or bug.

A negative result means your entered costs exceed your entered share; it does not prevent playing for enjoyment or practice. Use the beginner mission questions and cargo planning guide to consider the rest of the task before accepting it.
