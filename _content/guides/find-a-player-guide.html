Quick answer

The official Guide System connects players with volunteer teachers. Choose the skill you want to learn, request a guide and agree on a small lesson. It can be useful for returning players as well as first-time pilots.

Find the official Guide System

Open the official Guide Hub. RSI also documents access through Spectrum and the launcher in its Guide System instructions. Select a relevant skill and an available guide rather than sending an unexplained request.

According to RSI, a guide can accept or decline. An accepted request starts a Spectrum private conversation and sends a friend request. Availability is a person’s choice, so allow time for a response and keep your first message specific.

Ask for one useful outcome

“Teach me everything” is difficult to schedule. A narrower request lets both players judge what preparation is needed. These are examples you can adapt:

  • “I can reach my hangar, but I need help with the first takeoff and return landing.”
  • “I understand flying and want to try one beginner contract.”
  • “I am returning after a break and need help finding my equipment.”

Include your starting location, approximate time available and the point where you got stuck. Describe what you can already do so the lesson can begin at the right place.

Agree on a meeting plan

Before either person travels, confirm the game environment, whether you are together on the same server, and the exact meeting point. “At the spaceport” can leave two people waiting in different rooms. A named terminal area or hangar assignment is easier to recognize.

The party walkthrough covers contacts and joining. Keep the initial lesson simple: the fewer unfamiliar ships, destinations and tasks introduced at once, the easier it is to identify what you learned.

Use a show, try, repeat rhythm

Ask the guide to explain one step, then perform it yourself. Say what the interface shows before asking for the next instruction. If the screen differs, pause and compare the current state rather than repeatedly pressing a remembered shortcut.

After completing the task with help, repeat the smallest useful part independently. For landing, that might mean identifying the assignment and explaining the approach. For inventory, it might mean finding an item’s storage location and equipping it.

End with notes you can reuse

Write down the route, the action names you needed and any unresolved question. Close the guide session when finished. Your most useful takeaway is a process you can repeat, rather than a set of button presses without context.

Keep the plain-language glossary and departure checklist nearby for your next independent trip. This site is an independent written resource; guide requests belong in RSI’s official system.
