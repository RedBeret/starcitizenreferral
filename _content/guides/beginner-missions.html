Quick answer

Choose a first contract you understand and can equip for, rather than the largest displayed reward. Read its objective, destination, time limit and risks before accepting. There is no fixed beginner income rate promised by this guide.

Where to find contracts

RSI’s missions guide describes browsing the mobiGlas Contracts Manager, reading an offer, and accepting it into the accepted-mission list. Use the current in-game labels if the interface differs from the documentation.

Before looking for work, know how to retrieve your ship, leave its hangar and return. The first-flight route and quantum-travel guide cover those foundations.

Choose the job by its requirements

Contract typeQuestion to ask before accepting
Delivery or haulingWhat must move, does it fit, and how many destinations are involved?
InvestigationWhere must I search, and what conditions or opposition does the description mention?
Mercenary or bountyIs the task on foot or in a ship, and is the target another player?
CollectionDo I already have the requested items or a realistic way to obtain them?
Service beaconWhat assistance is requested, and can I actually provide it?

These are selection questions, not a guarantee that a category is safe. Read the individual offer. Check where your equipment is stored and understand your medical and regeneration options before accepting risky work. A small payout can still involve travel or equipment you have not learned to use.

The pre-departure checklist

  1. Objective: explain the completion condition in one sentence.
  2. Route: identify the pickup, task location and final destination.
  3. Equipment: confirm that the job suits the ship and tools you have available.
  4. Time: check any deadline against the session you can actually finish.
  5. Cost: consider fuel, supplies or other purchases before relying on the headline reward.
  6. Group: if working together, confirm that everyone accepted the shared contract.

For an early learning session, one completed and understood task is a useful outcome. Adding several unrelated missions before learning the first route can make a stalled objective much harder to diagnose.

Think in net aUEC, not only mission rewards

The arithmetic is simple: net gain = reward received − costs paid. For a planning example, a hypothetical 10,000 aUEC reward with 2,000 aUEC in expenses leaves 8,000 aUEC. Those are illustrative numbers, not a current mission payout or an hourly earnings claim.

Use your actual wallet and receipts when comparing sessions. Also record time spent retrieving the ship, travelling, buying supplies and recovering from problems. A large reward is not automatically the most productive task for your current setup.

Working with a party

RSI documents sharing accepted contracts and dividing the reward among party members who accept. Confirm participation before starting. Our friends guide separates contact setup, grouping and mission sharing.

Plan for setbacks

RSI says support cannot manually restore aUEC lost through a crash or bug. Its guidance also recommends avoiding putting all available funds into a single cargo haul. Keep your learning budget manageable and report reproducible issues through the official channels.

aUEC is the alpha-testing balance. It is distinct from the account’s UEC and the standard referral bonus; the currency explanation covers that distinction.
