Quick answer

Before moving cargo, separate a delivery contract from buying goods to resell. Then check the load, ship access, pickup and destination, handling steps and the funds you could lose. A large cargo number alone does not describe the whole job.

Hauling a contract and trading commodities

RSI’s contract guide describes delivery and hauling as moving an item to a specified place. Commodity trading is a different arrangement: goods can be acquired and sold where there is demand, as explained in the commodity reference.

Ask who owns the load and what counts as success. Delivering the right goods to the wrong place, or selling goods you were supposed to deliver under a contract, are different actions. Read the actual offer or terminal before purchasing anything.

Capacity is only one question

RSI describes ship cargo grids measured in SCU, or Standard Cargo Units, and specialized storage such as ore containers. Use the ship’s appropriate storage for the kind of material involved. Resources intended for processing do not all follow the same sale path as ordinary trade goods.

DestinationWhere does this particular load need to go?

The access and handling questions are practical planning prompts. Test them with a manageable load before accepting a job that depends on an untried arrangement. The ship-selection framework helps compare the complete workflow.

Plan the loading and return trip

Write the journey as pickup → loading → travel → unloading or hand-in → next stop. Account for the steps at both ends. A route that looks short on a map can still involve several trips between storage, the loading area and the cockpit.

For a group, assign who moves cargo, who operates the ship and who checks the destination. Agree on clear calls such as “loading complete” rather than assuming everyone has finished because one player is ready to fly.

Separate sales revenue from gain

For a trade, the sale amount is not the whole gain. Compare money received with the purchase cost and trip expenses. For a contract, compare the reward you actually receive with the costs you paid. Keep these records separate so a larger wallet movement is not mistaken for a larger profit.

RSI’s aUEC loss guidance recommends avoiding committing all available funds to a cargo haul. Use a learning load you can afford to lose. The mission-budget calculator helps compare a contract’s share and expenses; it does not predict trade prices.

Finish by checking the actual result

Read the completion or transaction state before leaving. For commodity terminals, RSI notes that the ship needs to have landed legally to appear for trading. Check the correct location and ship before assuming the load has vanished.

After the run, note what consumed time: loading, travel, waiting, finding the destination or returning. Improve the slowest understandable step before increasing the scale. Keep the departure checklist handy for the next trip.
