Quick answer

RSI lists 16 GB RAM and 150+ GB of SSD storage as minimums, and recommends 32 GB RAM. Those figures are only part of the requirements: your operating system, CPU instruction support and graphics capabilities also matter.

Minimum versus recommended specifications

Use the official RSI requirements as your starting point. This table records the published requirements checked on 15 November 2026.

ComponentMinimumRecommended
Operating system64-bit Windows 10, latest service packWindows 10, latest service pack, or Windows 11
CPUQuad core; Intel i7 Haswell or later, or AMD Excavator or laterQuad/eight core; Intel i7 Haswell or later, or AMD Ryzen 5 or later
GraphicsDirectX 11.1-compatible card, 4 GB VRAMDirectX 12-compatible card, 8 GB VRAM
Memory16 GB RAM32 GB DDR4 RAM
Storage150+ GB on an SSD150+ GB on an SSD

RSI additionally specifies AVX, AVX2 and FMA3 CPU instructions, plus graphics-driver support for Direct3D 11.1 and Vulkan 1.2. It does not list a native macOS or Linux release.

Check your own PC before buying

Make a short inventory of the machine you will actually use. Record the exact CPU and GPU models, installed RAM, Windows version, and which drive will hold the game. “Gaming laptop” or a processor family name alone is not enough to compare specifications.

  1. Identify the hardware. Use your system information and the hardware manufacturers’ specifications to check exact models and capabilities.
  2. Check the destination drive. Confirm that it is an SSD and inspect its free space. A large secondary hard drive does not meet the SSD requirement just because Windows itself is on an SSD.
  3. Compare every row. Extra RAM does not compensate for an unsupported processor instruction set or graphics feature.
  4. Plan for the download. Decide where the game will be installed before opening the launcher. See the installation guide for the path and verification checks.

Will meeting the requirements guarantee smooth performance?

No specific frame rate follows from a minimum-spec checklist. RSI notes that location, server population, background applications and network conditions can affect the experience. Treat the recommended column as a more useful comparison than a promise of a particular FPS.

For your own evaluation, repeat a short route on the same machine and settings. Note where slowdowns happen: loading into the game, walking through a city, or flying away from a busy location. That is more informative than comparing two screenshots taken in unrelated conditions.

Use a Free Fly to evaluate your setup

If an official Free Fly is running, it can be a useful opportunity to try the game before buying access. It still requires a suitable PC and the game download. Start with a manageable test session rather than planning a long trip immediately.

Follow the Free Fly access checklist, then use the first-flight guide for a concrete starting task. If you are comparing a laptop and desktop, record results separately; their cooling, storage and graphics configurations may differ even when their product names sound similar.
