The pilot’s field guide

Find an organization that fits your sessions.

Choose a community around how and when you actually play, then arrange a specific shared activity.

By PatchOpsSources checked 15 November 2026Independent community guide
Quick answer

A Star Citizen organization is a player community with its own identity and rules. Choose one by the people, activities and schedule you can actually join. Check its unique Spectrum ID, then use the official application or invitation process.

Organization, party and lesson: different purposes

RSI’s organization reference describes an org’s identity, charter, activities and membership. A party is the group you arrange for a session; an org is the wider community. Joining one does not arrange a meeting or explain a mission by itself.

If you mainly need help learning one task, the official Guide System may be a more direct first step. If you want familiar people for repeated sessions, compare communities around that goal.

Find and verify the group

Browse the official organization directory. Read the org’s public information, especially its rules and expected commitment. RSI notes that names can be shared by different groups, while the Spectrum ID identifies the particular organization.

Check that ID before applying or accepting an invitation. A familiar name or logo alone is not enough to tell two similarly presented groups apart. The official support article documents joining through an org page or an invitation, with eligibility based on being a backer or having a game package.

Questions that reveal whether it fits

TopicQuestion to ask
ScheduleWhen do people actually play, including the time zone?
ActivitiesWhat did the group do in its recent ordinary sessions?
ExperienceCan a new player take part with a starter ship?
CommunicationWhat voice or text participation is expected?
CommitmentAre attendance, ranks or roleplay optional or expected?

These are this site’s comparison questions, not an org rating system. A small group that plays at your available time can suit you better than a much larger group whose events you cannot attend.

Make the first session specific

Before meeting, agree on one activity, a starting location and the expected duration. Ask what equipment you need and which jobs are useful for a newcomer. Then use the party guide to coordinate the actual session.

Observe how the group explains unfamiliar steps and handles setbacks. One shared task tells you more about everyday play than an impressive recruitment description. You can make the next commitment after seeing whether the experience matches your expectations.

Review membership deliberately

RSI distinguishes a member’s rank from administrative roles that grant management abilities. Do not assume an impressive rank title gives a particular permission. Its article also documents leaving through the organization section of profile settings and blocking unwanted invitations through account options.

This page does not endorse or recruit for a particular org. Use its questions to find a community that works for you, then keep the practical session plan separate from the membership process.
