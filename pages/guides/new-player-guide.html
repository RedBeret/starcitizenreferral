Your first steps in Star Citizen.

A practical route from creating an account to taking your first flight. Start small, check the current requirements, and leave room to explore.

Before you spend anything

Star Citizen is still in alpha development. Expect bugs and changing systems. Start by reading the current event and release notes; a Free Fly can let you try the game before committing to a package.

Check your PC against RSI’s current requirements. Its published baseline includes 16 GB of RAM and 150+ GB of SSD storage; the recommendation is 32 GB of RAM. The full CPU, graphics and operating-system requirements matter too.

Create your RSI account

Open the official signup form and check that the referral field shows STAR-9L66-ZTDY. Complete account creation on RSI. This site never collects your password or payment information.

The normal referral benefit is 50,000 UEC. If you omitted a code, RSI allows 24 hours to add one in the account summary. Read the eligibility rules before choosing a code; it cannot be replaced once associated with your account.

Understand game access

Outside a Free Fly, you need a game package to play. A standalone ship is a different product and does not by itself include game access. Look for “Star Citizen Digital Download” in the package contents.

The current First Flight Pack is built around the Aurora Mk II. Check the live listing for its price, region, inclusions and any temporary loaners. Use our game-package checklist rather than choosing by the biggest ship.

Install and take your first steps

  1. Use the official RSI launcher and install the game on an SSD with sufficient free space.
  2. Choose the LIVE environment for the public game. PTU builds have separate access rules and downloads.
  3. Follow the current in-game onboarding and use RSI’s Welcome Hub for flight guidance.
  4. Start with a short session: learn your surroundings, reach the spaceport, retrieve your ship and practise a basic flight.

When a control or mission behaves differently from a guide, check the current patch and in-game prompts. Early development means old instructions can become misleading.

You do not need to learn alone. Use the official Welcome Hub and Guide System to find experienced players who can help with your first session.
